The documents behind the design.

The architecture paper makes the case and sets out the design. The specification fixes the state transition precisely enough to build a second implementation from it. The status document says what exists. Where the paper and the status document differ, the status document describes the code.

Neo-Blockchains: The Fold Architecture

Technical paper by ColdAI, 27 October 2026

Why blockchains did not move like AI, and a design that sells trust rather than ordering: two-sorted state, Linear Lanes and Contention Consensus, data availability, the Fold, the Assurance Algebra, Verifiable Clearing, Agent Charters, privacy and compliance by proof, the security model, the economics, six applications with their baselines, a phased plan with kill criteria, and open research problems.

The paper describes a system to be built. Its capacity and latency figures are design targets, and the reference implementation covers part of the design.

The state transition specification

Normative, version 1.3

The function a proof stack evaluates for every chunk of every Pulse, and through it the whole state transition: preparing and executing a transaction, every operation, the three phases of a Pulse, the commitments a chunk statement carries, verifying a chunk and folding a Pulse. Proof stack B was written from this document alone.

Version 1.3 adds validators, stake, epochs, rewards, slashing of equivocation, forced-inclusion obligations and committee rotation.
