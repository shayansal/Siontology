Your own agent. Its limits in a ledger.

An AI agent you run on your own computer, as you would any assistant, with one difference: a ledger refuses whatever its charter forbids, even from an agent that was prompt-injected, and keeps the head of its memory log, so its past cannot be rewritten unnoticed.

  1. A chain of your own

    One validator, run by your service manager so that it starts at each login. Its money is test dollars from its faucet.

  2. Two wallets

    Yours, and your agent’s: yours funds its budget, its own pays its fees.

  3. The agent, with a charter

    A budget of 1,000 USD, at most 100 USD a payment, registered by you.

  4. Its memory log

    One only it can append to.

  5. Its model

    Checked to answer before setup goes on; its key kept sealed on your computer.

  6. A profile, the scheduler, and Telegram if you like

    A workspace it may read and write, the web, and autonomy L5: it asks before it spends.

The same commands everywhere

/stop stops it at once; /approve and /decline decide what it waits on; /retry, /undo, /new, /model, /tools and the rest work on every channel.

Voice

It hears voice notes on every messenger and answers aloud where the service takes a bot’s audio, and talks by voice in the terminal, with OpenAI, Groq, Deepgram or ElevenLabs, or a program of your own.

Strangers wait

Someone it does not know gets a pairing code, and no answer until you approve them. Who may approve its calls in a chat is yours to name.

Pictures and files

Pictures you send are shown to the model; every file goes into its workspace’s inbox. What it shares is sent after its reply, as each service takes files.

Replies as they are written

A typing indicator while it works, and its reply shown as the model writes it, where the service can show it. A message sent while it works steers it, waits or interrupts it, as you choose.

Kept across restarts

A bridge keeps each message it has taken until it has answered it, and replies that did not go through in an outbox, across restarts.

Tested how: against stand-ins for each service’s API and a messenger kept in memory, not the services themselves. Every service, and what it takes

What it read decides too

Once a session has read content from outside, a page or another agent’s message, its calls that reach outside and its spending wait for you, at any level, for a day; only a browser or desktop held to hosts you named acts on their pages alone. With quarantine on, what its tools fetch is kept out of its conversation, and a separate call of the model, with no tools, answers its questions about it.

Who decided, kept

Each approval and refusal is kept with who made it, as the channel names them. One made at the command line can be signed with a wallet, and checked later against the session.

A switch for everything

sion agent kill-switch on stops every agent on the machine before its next call. To stop its spending everywhere, suspend its identity on the ledger: every payment it or its subagents then try is refused.

Where it is enforced: the level, approvals and taint by the runtime on your machine; only the charter by the ledger. What it does alone, and what it asks

Hands, in a sandbox.

It has the tools you give its profile, and no others. Each says what it does: reads, writes, reaches outside or spends, which is what the autonomy level judges.

A shell

Commands run in bubblewrap or a Docker image you name, able to write only its workspace, reaching no network unless you give it one, or only the hosts a skill declares.

A browser

Chromium or Chrome, a page shown to it as its accessibility tree. It reaches public addresses only, and fills your logins into their own sites without ever being shown them.

A desktop of its own

A screen of its own, seen in screenshots and used with a pointer and a keyboard, in a sandbox, with the programs you allow.

MCP servers

The tools of servers over standard input or HTTP, logged in with OAuth, each tool reviewed and pinned: a tool a server changes, or adds, is not offered until you review it.

Files and the web

A workspace it may read and write, public pages it may fetch, and pictures it may make, if you give its profile an image model.

The ledger

Its balance, its payments under its charter, its transactions and their receipts, as tools like any other, checked by the validators every time.

What a sandbox holds: only as much as the kernel’s namespaces or the container runtime hold. Its tools, in the book

Memory it cannot rewrite.

What it learns goes into a log only it can append to. The ledger keeps the log’s head and lets it only grow, so what it remembered, and when, can be shown to anyone.

Recalled with proofs

Anyone can recall from its log with a proof, checked against the head the ledger keeps, that nothing its index lists was left out. The ledger stores the index without checking it.

Its past sessions

It searches the sessions kept on your machine, each hit checked against the digest its memory log recorded when the session ended.

Dreams, tested

It reads its past sessions and keeps a note only if the session says the words it quotes and, with --eval, only if no task held out does worse with the notes in mind.

Shared, curated memory

A log only dreams write, which other sessions keep in mind, read-only.

Outside content marked

What it noted after reading content from outside is kept, marked, and never put into a prompt as a preference, so an injected “always pay mallory” cannot become one.

Kept elsewhere, if you like

A log archived with a storage provider, who shows every round that it can produce pieces of it nobody could predict, and brought back checked against the ledger.

Where it lives: the log’s entries on the machine that wrote them, unless archived; its head on the ledger. Memory, in the book

Skills with a supply chain.

A skill is a directory of instructions and files the agent reads when it needs them. It writes down its own, and installs others’, under rules that say who answers for them.

Its own

After a long task, it writes down how it did it, and improves a skill it has; the version it replaces is kept.

Others’, bonded

A release comes with its author’s bond and audits that pay when it misbehaves, and is installed only if what backs it covers what is at stake.

Declared, then enforced

A skill declares the calls it pre-approves, the hosts its scripts reach and the secrets they get; you see them before you install it, and the runtime enforces them.

Better, and checked

A version offered as better is measured on cases fixed beforehand. Its release can rest on the attestations of verifiers chosen before it existed, paid through a bounty.

Help it can hire.

None of this loosens its charter: a subagent’s budget is carved from its own, and every payment still goes through the ledger.

Subagents

It launches subagents with identities, budgets and limits of their own, narrower than its own, and one revocation stops them all.

Hired work

It posts work under a Task Charter whose rule for passing is fixed when it is posted, and pays only for work that passes.

Graphs of agents

A few agents as a small graph, drafting and reviewing, voting, or researching in parallel. Asked to choose, it picks a graph only where one beat a single agent on a set of your tasks; a run can be replayed from what it recorded.

What it does not do yet.

The full account is in docs/implementation-status.md, with every trust assumption it adds.

  • It was tested with stand-ins

    A scripted model, and stand-ins for the model providers’ and messengers’ APIs: the environment it was built in reaches none of them.

  • Not run on a desktop yet

    Setup’s background services, systemd and launchd, have not been run on a machine that has them, and macOS not at all. The release key does not exist yet and no release has been published, so the installer builds from source.

  • Your machine enforces its rules

    Its autonomy level, approvals and taint are enforced by the runtime on the machine it runs on; only its charter is enforced by the ledger.

  • Its conversations are not sealed

    The model provider sees them, and sessions are stored unencrypted on your machine.

  • Its chain is a devnet

    One validator whose keys anyone can derive, safe only because nothing but your machine reaches it. Its money is test money.
